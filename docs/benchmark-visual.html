Analysis

Correctness of the comparison: both boots verified by registry marks — the locked boot read StartEnable=0 (pass-through), the unlocked boot ran the full tower to phase 13 with selector verification. Same CUDA 13.4 build on both sides.

Stability of the unlock: unlocked trace mean 33.75 TFLOPS, coefficient of variation 5.7% across consecutive runs at a steady 1965 MHz — no TDRs, no WHEA errors, no clock anomalies attributable to the unlock over the entire session. The unlock state is digital (selector registers), not analog: it does not degrade, drift, or half-apply.

Volatility of the unlock (by design): the selectors live in a power-gated domain — any power removal (shutdown, reboot, sleep) clears them. The armed filter re-establishes the state at every GPU start, making it effectively persistent across reboots while remaining physically volatile. Disarming returns the card to stock at the next boot with zero residue: nothing is flashed, patched, or written to persistent storage.

Thermal note: the locked trace's late-run droop (9 runs at 0 MHz) is board thermal throttling after the sustained suite on a 46 °C-start card — a card property, present locked or unlocked; suite medians (first 4 runs, pre-throttle) are the comparable figures.

Bottom line: on a 200 W board, expect the unlock as a +10–12% end-to-end gain on compute-bound training math (2048–4096 GEMMs) and a wash on power-saturated large batches — not the +68–80% headline numbers, which belong to 350–450 W boards where the rate cap, not the power cap, is the binding constraint.
